# Illustration brief: Paul's Python Course

Thank you for considering this commission. This brief is everything you need to quote for and draw the art; nothing here assumes you've seen the project. Questions are welcome at any point, and the owner, Paul Nercessian, will answer them before you start.

## 1. The project

Paul's Python Course is a paid web course that teaches Python from a learner's first line of code all the way to machine learning and reinforcement learning, one real project at a time (a bill splitter, a weather app, a spam filter, a game-playing AI). Learners write and run real code in the browser, guided by a small cast of illustrated people: Paul, the professor; Neil, his teaching assistant; and four classmates who bring the projects. The school lives in an old brick clocktower, and each floor is a stage of the course that fills with light and people as the learner gets further.

Live site: **[link to follow before launch: https://paulspythoncourse.example]**

The interface around your art is a clean, modern, dark code editor with one strong accent colour (a Python yellow-orange, `#FFB627`). The interface stays quiet on purpose: the warmth and personality of the product come from your characters and the Clocktower.

### Where the art appears

| Where | What | Shown at |
|---|---|---|

| A project's first screen | The classmate who wants the program, half-body, asking for it | 360 to 512 px tall |

Portraits are often cropped to a circle and filled behind with the character's signature colour (listed with each character below), so a portrait must read well on its own colour and stay recognisable at 48 px.

## 2. The style

- **2D hand-drawn illustration.** Clean, confident linework with slight variation in line weight. Flat colour with one shadow tone and an occasional soft highlight. A warm, slightly muted palette that sits well next to a dark navy-black interface and the yellow-orange accent.
- **Semi-realistic, stylised proportions:** about 6.5 to 7 heads tall for a full figure.
- **Faces carry the charm:** expressive eyes and eyebrows, and face shapes and hairstyles distinct enough that every character is recognisable in silhouette and at 48 px.
- **Clothing tells you who they are** (see the cast below). Each character has an everyday outfit and, for the group illustration only, an optional matching school-uniform outfit: a pale grey-blue short-sleeved shirt.
- **Consistent across the whole cast:** the same line weight, the same shading approach, light from the top left, one palette family.
- For the feeling (not to copy): the illustrated warmth of games such as Florence, Spiritfarer and Unpacking.

**Please avoid:** chibi proportions; anime eyes; 3D rendering or a 3D look; corporate vector people (in the style of Humaaans or Open Peeps); gradients; glossy highlights; pixel art. The owner doesn't want AI-generated or AI-assisted imagery, and characters must stay consistent from pose to pose.

## 3. The cast

All six characters are based on real people the owner knows. **Please draw them warmly and respectfully**: friendly and natural, not idealised into models and not caricatured. They are young adults, late teens to about twenty. Never exaggerate or comment on body shape. The owner will send reference photos to you directly; please don't publish or share them. Each person will see their character before launch, so allow for one round of small likeness changes per character.

`art/pixel-reference/` in the project (the owner can send you `cast-sheet.png`) has rough pixel portraits drawn from these descriptions. Use them only as a quick guide to colours and hair. They aren't the style, and they don't match the real faces. Where a reference and the words in this brief differ, the words win.

## 4. Deliverables

187 images in all: **152 required** and 35 optional. Every one is listed by file name in `art/manifest.json` (the owner can send you a spreadsheet of it), with a note on what to draw.

### 4.1 Portraits: 61

Half-body (waist up), facing the viewer or slightly turned, for each of the six characters, in **ten expressions**:

| Expression | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| neutral | At rest and friendly, looking at the viewer |
| talking | Mid-sentence, mouth open, explaining something |
| pleased | Happy with what they see on the screen |
| proud | Something they made works, and they know it |
| thinking | Working something out, eyes up and to the side |
| confused | Brows knitted, head tilted |
| worried | Brows up in the middle, mouth tight |
| surprised | Brows up, eyes wide, mouth open |
| laughing | A real laugh, eyes creased |
| unimpressed | Flat mouth, one brow lower: not convinced |

6 characters x 10 expressions = 60, plus **Paul laughing in his gold paper crown** for the final scene = 61. Remember the personal moments: Paul pushing his cap back or rubbing his neck (thinking), Neil pushing up his glasses (thinking), Riya's finger heart (pleased), Yahya's peace sign (proud), and Yahya's default look, which is cool rather than smiling.

### 4.4 The Clocktower: 2 building layers and 54 floor layers

An old brick clocktower that Paul has turned into a code school, drawn as a **cross-section, like a dollhouse**, so every floor is visible at once. Each floor is a separate layer in **three states**:

- **locked:** dark, the furniture under dust sheets, no people;
- **open:** lights on, furniture uncovered, ready for work, no people;
- **busy:** the residents there and doing something, more props, plants grown.

From the bottom up:

| Layer name | Floor | Room | Who's there when it's busy |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ground` | Ground floor | The Workshop: Paul's classroom, a big desk, a chalkboard, the duck | Paul |
| `floor1` | Floor 1 | The Studio: desks, shelves of notebooks, a kettle | Neil |
| `floor2` | Floor 2 | The Makerspace: a soldering bench, a small arcade cabinet, a server under the desk | Caitlin |
| `floor3` | Floor 3 | The Puzzle Room: a wall of sticky notes and string, a chess table | Yahya |
| `floor4` | Floor 4 | The Archive: filing cabinets, a huge wall chart, a weather station outside the window | Aaesha |
| `floor5` | Floor 5 | The Model Room: whiteboards full of graphs, a coffee machine | Riya |
| `floor6` | Floor 6 | The Engine Room: the clock's gears behind glass, a rack of glowing servers | Aaesha and Neil |
| `floor7` | Floor 7 | The Arcade: a row of cabinets, a leaderboard, a prize shelf | Yahya and Caitlin |
| `roof` | The roof | The clock face and the roof terrace: the city skyline, string lights | Everyone |

The clock face is stuck at 3:14 in the roof's locked and open states. In the roof's **busy** state the clock runs again at last: string lights on, the whole class there, Paul in his gold paper crown.

Plus the **building layer** (`clocktower_shell`): the sky, the street, the brick walls and the roof line around the floors.

- **Night version: required** (27 floor layers and the building).
- **Day version: optional** (another 27 and the building), daylight through the windows. If there's only a night version, the app uses it in both its dark and light themes.
- Locked floors must look deliberately dark and covered, not unfinished: no noise or dither textures.

## 5. Technical specs

| Image | Canvas (pixels) | Background |
|---|---|---|
| Portraits | 1024 x 1024 | Transparent |
| Full-body poses | 1024 x 2048 | Transparent |
| Group illustration | 2400 x 1600 | Opaque (a full scene) |
| Clocktower building | 2400 x 4000 | Opaque |
| Clocktower floors | 2400 x 400 (the roof 2400 x 800) | Opaque; transparent outside the room is welcome |
| Avatar parts | 1024 x 1024 | Transparent |
| Shelf objects | 512 x 512 | Transparent |
| The duck | 512 x 512 | Transparent |

Every exported file must be **exactly** its canvas size. Transparent means the background is fully transparent: the project's check fails a file with a white or coloured background, or with none of the canvas left clear.

**Templates** (please send these with the style sketch so everyone works to them):

- **Portraits:** the face centred left to right, the **eye line at y = 400**, the top of the head at about y = 150 to 200, the waist at the bottom edge. Everything that makes the character recognisable, including hands making the finger heart or the peace sign, stays **inside the circle that fills the canvas**, because the app crops portraits to that circle at small sizes. The same head size and eye line for every character and every expression, so they line up when they swap.
- **Full body:** the character centred, **feet on a shared baseline at y = 1980**, the same scale for everyone.
- **Avatar parts:** drawn on the portrait template, so every part lines up when stacked: back hair, face, features, eyebrows, facial hair, hair, glasses, top, accessory.
- **Clocktower:** one master at 2400 x 4000 with the roof at the top (y 0 to 800) and the eight floors below it, 400 px each: floor 7 at y 800 to 1200, down to the ground floor at y 3600 to 4000. Export each floor in each state cropped to its band, and the building layer at full size. Keep every floor's walls and floor line in the same place in all three states, so states can swap without anything jumping.

**Files:**

- Exports: PNG, sRGB, 8-bit per channel with alpha (16-bit is fine), at the master size.
- Sources: layered files (PSD, Procreate or Clip Studio) with the layers named. SVG is welcome as well if the style allows it.
- **Naming** matches `art/manifest.json`: the character or thing, the kind, then the expression, pose or state, separated by underscores, all lower case. Hyphens stay as they are.

|---|---|
| Paul's portrait, pleased | `paul_portrait_pleased.png` |

## 7. Milestones

1. **Style sketch, for approval:** Paul and one other character (Caitlin suggested), half-body, neutral, in finished style and colour, with the portrait template. Nothing else starts until this is approved.
2. **All 61 portraits**, delivered character by character, so each can be checked with the real person as it lands.
3. **The rest:** the 12 full-body poses and the group illustration; the Clocktower (night, then day if it's in the quote); the avatar parts; the 13 Shelf objects and the duck.

Each milestone gets one round of feedback, plus one round of small likeness changes per character once the people themselves have seen it. Please quote per milestone, with the day version of the Clocktower and the optional avatar layers priced separately.

## 8. Delivering the files

Send the PNGs named as above, in one folder per milestone, and the layered sources alongside. The owner drops the PNGs into the project, runs one check (it confirms each file's size and transparent background), and they appear in the app straight away. If a file fails the check, you'll get the exact reason, for example "is 1000 x 1000 pixels; it should be 1024 x 1024".
