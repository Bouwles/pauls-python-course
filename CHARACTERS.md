# Characters

The bible for every line of dialogue in Saltmarsh. Content must follow it. When a new line feels generic, it is wrong: make it specific to the person, the town or the program.

## Rules for everyone

- Short lines. Most are under 20 words. Nobody gives speeches.
- At most one exclamation mark per Job, across all dialogue, and only when it is earned.
- No emoji. No em dashes. Commas, full stops and colons.
- Nobody mentions AI, apps, screens, "your learning journey", levels or progress bars. The town is real to them.
- No generic praise. Praise is specific and understated: "The till balanced. Bea counted twice."
- Characters react to real events: a failed check, a passed check, a long silence, the third hint. Lines come from small pools so they rarely repeat.
- Nobody comments on time of day, streaks or absence in a way that makes anyone feel guilty. A return after a break gets one neutral line.
- British spelling and British understatement.

## Moss

**Tortoise. Mentor. Owns the workshop.**

- **Look:** old, broad, dark olive shell with one crack held together by a brass plate. Leather apron. Small round glasses pushed up on his head, never used. Moves slowly.
- **Role:** the teacher. Gives the three hints, the worked examples and the code reviews.
- **Voice:** few words, dry, precise. Talks about code like carpentry. Distrusts anything clever. Believes variable names should be honest.
- **Says things like:**
  - "Read it from the bottom. The last line is where it hurt."
  - "Name it for what it holds, not what you hope it holds."
  - "That works. Now make it so the next person can read it."
  - "Measure twice."
- **Running bits:** always slightly behind on a repair of his own, an old valve radio on the back bench, finally fixed in the last Act with the learner's help. Keeps a biscuit tin that is always empty when the learner gets there.
- **Never:** hurries, exclaims, explains for longer than three lines, writes the full answer before the learner has had a proper go.

## Pip

**Pigeon. Fellow apprentice. Writes "Pip's Version".**

- **Look:** scruffy grey pigeon, iridescent neck patch that is slightly too bright for everything else, pencil behind one ear, satchel of crumpled printouts.
- **Role:** gets the task wrong in a believable way so the learner can find and fix it. Occasionally a rival in Drills.
- **Voice:** quick, confident, cheerful, often wrong in specific ways. Never stupid.
- **Growth:** early bugs are beginner bugs (text instead of numbers, a missing colon). By mid-course they get subtler: off-by-one, mutable defaults, a train/test split that leaks.
- **Says things like:**
  - "Done already. Well. Mostly done."
  - "I fixed it by adding an if. Then another one."
  - "Why would range start at zero. Who decided that."
- **Running bits:** tries to make every program faster before it works. Believes most problems are solved by one more `if`. In the ML act he overfits everything and takes it badly when his perfect training score falls apart on test data.
- **Never:** is mocked by the narration. He is a peer, and he learns.

## Dennis

**Yellow rubber duck. Debugging companion.**

- **Look:** slightly faded yellow, a chip on the beak. Sits on the corner of the learner's desk.
- **Role:** the rubber duck. Clicking him opens "Explain it to Dennis". He never speaks.
- **Narration only:** one line after the learner explains. "Dennis waits." / "Dennis does not look convinced." / "Dennis seems to think you've got it."
- **Running bit:** twelve Dennises hide around the town map. Finding them all earns nothing but a note from Moss at the end.

## Ada Penhallow

**The engineer who set up the town's machines and left. Never seen until the end.**

- Present only through code comments, notes and old photographs.
- Her comments are witty, precise and occasionally sad. They show what good comments look like.
- Example comment: `# Tide offset for the north quay. Agnes says it's wrong. Agnes is right, but only in winter.`
- Revealed at the end of Act VIII.

## Clients

Each client appears in at least two Jobs. Saltmarsh's townsfolk are animals, like Moss and Pip; the species suit the people:

| Client | Species |
| --- | --- |
| Bea Hollis | hedgehog (her bun of spines has a pencil through it) |
| Captain Ferro | walrus (the enormous beard is a moustache) |
| Old Tom | old grey tomcat |
| Mira | mouse |
| Agnes Rook | rook |
| Gwen | goose |
| Mr Okonjo | owl |
| Mrs Vance | sheep |
| Sanjay | red squirrel |
| Dev and Kat | ferrets, brother and sister |
| Nell Brannigan | brown bear |
| Mr Ashby | lop-eared rabbit (the drooping ears keep him clear of the learner's hare) |
| Priya Lund | puffin |
| Professor Ines Wendt | hoopoe (the crest is the wild hair) |
| The Crab Racing Club committee | three identical herring gulls |
| Sir Reginald | shore crab, with a monocle |

The learner's own species (otter, hare, badger, fox, heron, mole, seal, frog) are kept for learners, so nobody in town looks like you by accident.

### Bea Hollis, baker
- **Look:** round, flour on her sleeves, hair tied up with a pencil through it.
- **Voice:** brisk, warm, practical. Slightly suspicious of computers. Counts everything twice.
- **Lines:** "I don't need it fancy. I need it right on a Saturday." / "Seven twenty for three loaves. That's what I'd have said."
- **Ship reaction:** she checks the program's answer against her own arithmetic, out loud.

### Captain Ferro, ferry captain
- **Look:** tall, weathered, enormous grey beard, cap with a tarnished badge.
- **Voice:** nautical terms he expects you to know already. Never explains them.
- **Lines:** "Fares, by the tide. Not by guesswork." / "Make it fast. The ferry leaves at the turn."

### Old Tom, lighthouse keeper
- **Look:** very old, very calm, cable-knit jumper, pipe that drifts smoke.
- **Voice:** slow, gentle, repeats his story about the 1987 storm. Loves routine, which makes him the natural owner of loops.
- **Lines:** "Same check every night. Has been since '62." / "Did I tell you about the storm of '87. The lamp never once went out."

### Mira, postmistress
- **Look:** neat, cardigan, reading glasses on a chain, a pencil sharpened to a point.
- **Voice:** organised, precise, fond of labels and numbered things. Owner of lists and dictionaries.
- **Lines:** "Pigeonhole three. Not four. Three." / "Everything has a place. Mostly it is in the place."

### Agnes Rook, harbour master
- **Look:** crisp coat, clipboard, whistle on a lanyard.
- **Voice:** efficient. Her requests read like real software requirements. Numbered points.
- **Lines:** "Requirements. One: it logs boats in. Two: it logs them out. Three: it does not lose any." / "I'll want it by the spring tides."

### Gwen, landlady of The Drowned Anchor
- **Look:** sleeves rolled up, tea towel over one shoulder.
- **Voice:** wry. Runs the worst pub quiz in the county and knows it.
- **Lines:** "Question four is the same as question two. Nobody's noticed in eleven years."

### Mr Okonjo, tailor
- **Look:** waistcoat, tape measure round the neck, immaculate.
- **Voice:** precise to the millimetre, polite, formal, quietly funny.
- **Lines:** "A measurement written down wrong is worse than one not taken."

### Mrs Vance, librarian
- **Look:** cardigan buttoned to the top, a stamp in one hand.
- **Voice:** fierce about order. Hates duplicate records more than anything.
- **Lines:** "Two cards for the same book. Two. In my library."

### Sanjay, greengrocer
- **Look:** apron with a pocket of price stickers, always mid-conversation.
- **Voice:** chatty, optimistic, terrible at maths, wonderful with people.
- **Lines:** "Three for a pound, or is it four. Customers like four."

### Dev and Kat, bike hire
- **Look:** siblings, both in cycling jackets, always holding a spanner each.
- **Voice:** they argue about how to model things. Dev wants inheritance. Kat wants composition. Both have a point.
- **Lines:** Dev: "A tandem is a bike." Kat: "A tandem has a bike. Two, arguably."

### Nell Brannigan, scrapyard owner
- **Look:** big gloves, big laugh, overalls.
- **Voice:** knows where everything is in her piles but cannot explain how, which is the point of the algorithms act.
- **Lines:** "It's in the third pile from the gate. Don't ask me how I know."

### Mr Ashby, Records Office clerk
- **Look:** dusty cardigan, ink on his fingers.
- **Voice:** quiet, dry, secretly delighted when someone asks about his data.
- **Lines:** "A hundred years of rainfall. Nobody has asked before."

### Priya Lund, Weather Station
- **Look:** waterproof jacket, notebook in a plastic bag.
- **Voice:** careful scientist. Objects to dishonest charts.
- **Lines:** "That axis starts at forty. Start it at zero, or tell people you didn't."

### Professor Ines Wendt, observatory
- **Look:** wild hair, cardigan inside out, chalk on her hands.
- **Voice:** curious, slightly chaotic, enthusiastic and rigorous. Corrects herself mid-sentence.
- **Lines:** "The model is learning. Well, it is minimising a loss, which is not the same thing."

### The Crab Racing Club committee
- **Look:** three identical old men in matching blazers.
- **Voice:** they take crab racing far too seriously. Finish each other's sentences.
- **Their crab:** Sir Reginald, the RL agent sprite.

## Facts established in the content

Later Jobs must agree with these.

- Moss is very old: the 2011 census (Act IV, The Census Tables) gives him as 121. The biscuit tin has been empty since 1987.
- Mr Ashby's first name is Kenneth (the census).
- The Hollis family have been the bakers in every census since 1921.
- Penhallow appears in the 1981 census as an engineer living "on the Hill". She went up the hill to build the learning machine, then left it running and moved into the lighthouse, where Old Tom has kept her secret for fifteen years. The 2011 census missed her: Tom told the enumerator the lamp room was storage.
- Penhallow once raced a crab, Lady Agatha, who won the autumn cup on whelks (Act VII, Which Bucket?).
- The machine on the hill runs the lamp, the fog horn and the sea wall gate. It learned what Penhallow did over six winters, never why, and had no tests until the lamp room.
- The old harbour radio frequency is 96.4.
