  - id: cited
    text: "The paper is cited in full: authors, title, journal, volume and year."

  - id: rerun
    text: "Someone else could rerun everything from your instructions and get the same figures, seeds and all."
    why: "A reproduction nobody else can reproduce has rather missed the point."
  - id: traced
    text: "Every claim in the write-up points to a figure or a number your code produced."
    why: "If it can't be pointed at, it's an opinion. Opinions go in their own paragraph, labelled."
  - id: suggests
    text: "No sentence says 'proves'; the size of your experiment is stated plainly."
    why: "Ten seeds on a made-up problem with forty examples suggest. Say suggest, and say how small it was."

The write-up is for someone who has read the paper and is deciding whether to trust your reproduction.

1. First paragraph: what you reproduced, at what scale, and how closely it matched.
2. The main figure, and how you made it.
3. Your own experiment: the prediction, and what happened.
4. What didn't match, and your best guess why, marked as a guess. The paper's experiments were bigger, with real data; yours fits on a laptop. This is the section Paul reads first.
5. The paper cited in full, and how to rerun everything.
