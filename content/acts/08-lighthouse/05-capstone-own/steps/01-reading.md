Three questions sort the good ideas from the rest.

**Who is it for?** Name a person. "People who like weather" can't tell you you're wrong; Priya can. A real user tells you what matters, and when you're done. It can be you, if you'll use it.

**What does done look like?** Write the moment it's finished: "Mira types a weight and gets the price." If you can't describe that moment, you can't reach it. Then halve it: first versions are always smaller than you think.

**What can be tested?** Keep the decisions, the sums and the rules in plain functions that take values and give values back. Screens, networks and keyboards go round the outside. Then most of the project can be checked by tests, as Gwen's rules were.

If the hard part is something you've never done, that's a risk, not a reason to stop. Write it down first.
